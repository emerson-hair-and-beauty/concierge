Hair snapping when detangling

my hair keeps snapping when i detangle and theres little broken pieces all over my shoulders
What we decided
Her hair is breaking. Rebuild strength before styling anything.

Answer 1 of 4

You are seeing breakage during detangling, with small pieces collecting on your shoulders. This is not normal shedding. It points to your hair’s internal structure taking strain. When curls snap this way, the root cause is nearly always a weakness in the hair fibre itself. For you, this is not about needing more moisture. The breakage is happening because the strands have lost strength and elasticity. Tight curls with low porosity can struggle to absorb what they need. When protein gets out of balance, or the cuticle is stressed by repeated swelling and drying, you get what’s called hygral fatigue. That means the hair loses its ability to bounce back under tension, so it snaps instead of stretching.

Your next step is to bring protein and repair to the centre of your routine. Prioritise treatments that actually bond to the hair and rebuild its structure. Look for ingredients like hydrolyzed protein, which is protein broken down small enough to fit into the cuticle and reinforce weak spots. Avoid heavy butters and moisture-focused masks at this stage. They sit on low porosity hair and make the strands feel soft temporarily, but they do not address the underlying weakness.

Keep manipulation to a minimum while your hair recovers. Detangle only when wet and coated with a product that has slip. Work in sections and use your fingers or a wide-tooth comb, never a brush. Low porosity hair takes time to recover because the cuticle is tight, so protein needs to be part of your routine for several weeks, not just a single treatment.

This approach matters because adding more moisture right now would only make things worse. Your hair’s problem is not dryness, it is a lack of structure. Protein is what reverses this kind of breakage. Once you see fewer snapped pieces and your hair feels springy again, you can start balancing moisture back in. For now, repair comes first.
