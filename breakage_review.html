Answer 1 of 2

When you see snapping during detangling and small broken hairs collecting on your shoulders, the problem is deeper than dryness or tangling. This is structural weakness. Your hair is breaking at points of stress because the internal bonds are compromised. For high porosity curls like yours, the damage usually builds up from repeated moisture swelling and contracting the hair shaft. This cycle, called hygral fatigue, disrupts the cuticle and leaves the core of the strand vulnerable.

The broken pieces you’re noticing are not shed hairs. They are mid-strand breaks. This always points to a need for repair, not just softness. Moisture will not solve this stage of damage. If your routine has been focused on hydration or rich creams, pause that approach. What your hair lacks now is structure. The answer is protein. Protein treatments work by filling the gaps in the cuticle, reinforcing weak spots, and helping the strand resist further breakage when you style or detangle.

Start with a targeted protein mask, something designed to strengthen rather than coat. Use it with low manipulation: detangle gently with your fingers or a wide-tooth comb, only when your hair is saturated with product. Follow the instructions closely. Overusing protein can make the hair brittle, but right now your hair is showing every sign of needing reinforcement.

This takes priority over moisture because in high porosity hair, repeated moisture without structure accelerates the cycle of breakage. Repair first, then rebalance with a lighter leave-in. Wait until you see fewer broken pieces before you return to richer hydration.

The reason this beats any alternative is clear. When mid-strand breakage is happening, no amount of oil, butter, or deep conditioning will restore strength. Only targeted repair will stop the snapping you’re seeing. Address the root cause now, and your curls will regain the resilience you need for healthy styling and less fallout.

Answer 2 of 2

Snapping during detangling and broken pieces on your shoulders are both classic signs of structural weakness in your hair. For you, this is not about dryness alone. High porosity curls like yours lose protein and moisture more easily, but when breakage shows up this clearly, the core issue is damage in the structure itself. This means your hair’s internal bonds are compromised, making each strand less able to flex and bounce back under tension.

The first step is to focus on repair, not extra moisture. Right now, adding more hydration-heavy products will only make the strands feel softer for a moment, without restoring their strength. Instead, your hair needs protein to reinforce those weakened bonds. Look for treatments with hydrolyzed protein. This is protein broken down small enough to penetrate the hair shaft and rebuild from within. Work these into your routine gently and consistently. Avoid heavy manipulation when your hair is wet, because high porosity hair is most fragile then.

The reason this approach outranks moisture or even oil-based treatments is because your hair’s foundation is compromised. Until you restore some internal strength, nothing else will stick. Moisture alone will just wash out or evaporate, and oils will coat the surface without addressing the real problem. Repair first, then you can circle back to your usual curl care.

If you want a starting point, focus on regular protein treatments and keep your detangling gentle and intentional. Take your time and use your fingers or a wide-tooth comb, always with plenty of slip. This makes a bigger difference for high porosity curls than chasing moisture alone. Once you see fewer broken pieces and feel your hair resisting less, you’ll know the structure is coming back.
